Meet Stacy W. Smallwood, PhD, MPH

Executive Director, Wake Forest University School of Divinity's Faith COMPASS Center


Dr. Stacy W. Smallwood began serving as executive director on June 1, 2024. He previously held the role of associate professor of community health and founding director of the Office of Health Equity and Community Engagement in the Jiann-Ping Hsu College of Public Health at Georgia Southern University. He is also an affiliate faculty member in the university’s women’s, gender and sexuality studies program.


Dr. Smallwood has dedicated his career to advancing HIV prevention and treatment, especially in rural and underserved communities. His work includes developing faith-based interventions in South Carolina and leading efforts to expand awareness and access to pre-exposure prophylaxis, or PrEP, across Georgia.


He is a past chair of the American Public Health Association’s HIV/AIDS Section, where he established a fellowship program for early-career HIV researchers. He currently serves on the HIV Prevention Trials Network 096 protocol team, which focuses on stigma, equity and PrEP access for Black gay, bisexual and same-gender-loving men in the American South. He is also a member of the PrEP in Black America coalition, which advocates for a national PrEP agenda to support Black communities.


Dr. Smallwood has published more than 30 scholarly articles, essays and book chapters. He is principal investigator for Racial Equity to Advance Community Health (REACH) Bulloch and co-investigator for the Safe Water Together initiative in Brunswick, Georgia.


He holds a doctorate and Master of Public Health from the University of South Carolina and a bachelor’s degree in health and exercise science from Wake Forest University. He is also an ordained minister in the Christian Methodist Episcopal Church.

Biomedical HIV Prevention Summit Returns to Atlanta


The ninth annual Biomedical HIV Prevention Summit, hosted by NMAC (formerly the National Minority AIDS Council), was held in Atlanta from April 10-12, 2025. Since its founding in 2016, the summit has focused on translating biomedical advancements—such as PrEP and HIV treatment innovations—into accessible prevention strategies for the communities most impacted by HIV.


This year’s event brought together a diverse group of participants, including representatives from federal and state agencies, the HIV workforce, the healthcare sector and PrEP users and advocates. Together, they shared insights, strategies and new knowledge to advance health equity and improve access to prevention tools.


The Satcher Health Leadership Institute (SHLI) participated by tabling throughout the event and providing attendees with project information and educational resources. The summit served as a powerful reminder of the importance of collaboration and informed action in the ongoing fight to end HIV.

PrEP in Black America Strategy Convening Launches Research Agenda

On April 8, Atlanta hosted a landmark moment in HIV prevention: the inaugural PrEP in Black America (PIBA) Strategy Convening and the launch of the first-ever National Black HIV Prevention Research Agenda.


PIBA is a coalition of Black researchers, advocates, activists and policymakers dedicated to advancing HIV prevention efforts in Black communities across the country. The convening sought to:
  1. Energize the Black public health workforce;
  2. Educate Black communities on the science and effectiveness of PrEP; and
  3. Support the development of a federally funded National PrEP program.


This milestone represented a critical leap forward in promoting health equity through evidence-based solutions driven by community knowledge and leadership. SHLI was honored to contribute its voice to the conversation and support the call for community-led innovation and policy change.

MSM Family Medicine Retreat Highlights HIV Awareness

On April 2, the Department of Family Medicine at Morehouse School of Medicine hosted its annual retreat, sponsored by ViiV Healthcare. SHLI helped organize an insightful session on HIV stigma and prevention, led by Dr. Lawrence Bryant and HIV Equity Task Force members Beautiful Devynne and Masonia Traylor.


The session explored the role of PrEP, the impact of HIV stigma on care access and how increasing provider knowledge can directly benefit patient care.

"UNexpected" Screening Centers HIV Awareness and Testing

On March 27, EHE HIV Equity Task Force member Masonia Traylor hosted a free community screening of her powerful documentary "UNexpected" at Regal Cinema Atlantic Station. The film highlights personal stories of resilience and advocacy in the HIV community.


SHLI proudly partnered for the event, distributing more than 60 free at-home HIV test kits to attendees. The event was a moving reminder that stories can be a catalyst for education, testing and ultimately, transformation.

Black Family Wellness Expo Champions Community Health

The Atlanta Cluster of The Links, Inc. hosted the 2025 Black Family Wellness Expo at Morehouse School of Medicine on March 15. The event aimed to reduce health and economic disparities and improve quality of life in Black communities across Atlanta and Fulton County.


The expo brought together health advocates, professionals and community members committed to promoting equitable outcomes in health and education. The Satcher Health Leadership Institute (SHLI) proudly participated, sharing vital information about the Ending the HIV Epidemic (EHE) initiative. SHLI team members engaged in meaningful dialogue with attendees, underscoring the importance of HIV education and prevention while exchanging knowledge to help shape a healthier future.
