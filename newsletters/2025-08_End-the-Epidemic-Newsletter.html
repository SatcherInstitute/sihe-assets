Meet Elder Will Francis

Pastor, strategist and public health advocate


Elder Will Francis is a pastor, strategist and public health advocate whose life’s work sits at the intersection of faith, health equity and social transformation. Guided by his conviction that “anything we build must feed bodies, heal communities and govern systems,” Francis has spent more than two decades dismantling disparities and creating frameworks where justice and healing flow together.


As Senior Director of Community Engagement at Access Resources & Community, Inc. (ARC), Francis leads efforts to connect health, church and community ecosystems. He pioneered the rollout of FaithRx, a catalytic initiative that equips churches to serve as health and wellness hubs through pop-up clinics, referral networks and healing-centered ministry. Under his leadership, FaithRx has become a blueprint for addressing health disparities by positioning faith-based spaces as the front door of public health.


Beyond ARC, Francis is the founder and apostolic leader of The 166 Church, a movement-driven hub designed to reach “Beyond Sunday.” Through initiatives such as Mission ATL, The 166 tackles poverty, housing insecurity and health inequities with both biblical strategy and practical solutions.


﻿His impact is shaped by both rigorous training and lived experience. With a master’s degree in Practical Theology, extensive public health certifications and ongoing participation in apostolic and leadership cohorts, Francis brings intellectual depth and cultural fluency to every space he enters. Whether teaching in a pulpit, building strategy in a boardroom or breaking down HIV prevention in a barbershop, Francis embodies the role of a systems thinker for social change committed to building infrastructures that heal, not harm.

We asked Elder Francis: What three things fuel you most in this season?

Building Systems that Heal, Not Harm


“I wake up motivated by the mandate to design ecosystems where faith, health equity and justice converge. Every blueprint I create must outlive me.”

The Urgency of Community Transformation


“Lives are literally hanging in the balance. The urgency of responding prophetically and practically keeps me relentless.”

Legacy and Multiplication


“It’s not just about what I build, but who I build for. Raising leaders and multiplying movements is my assignment.”

On Monday, August 18, the Satcher Health Leadership Institute (SHLI) teamed up with the Atlanta University Center Consortium Student Health and Wellness Center (AUCC-SHWC) to host a learning session for Morehouse College’s incoming freshmen. Community partner Tyson Randolph led the conversation, encouraging students to make informed choices about their sexual health, respect the rights of others and take steps to prevent HIV and other sexually transmitted infections (STIs).


The SHLI table quickly became a gathering spot for students who not only picked up giveaway items but also engaged in meaningful conversations about navigating campus life with confidence and responsibility.


Big thanks to the staff and volunteers for your support!
