Meet Shakera Cheston

CHOICE Project Coordinator at Morris Brown College


Shakera Cheston serves as the Project Coordinator and Lead Navigator for the CHOICE Project at Morris Brown College. She is a proud alumna of Fort Valley State University, where she graduated with a bachelor's degree in Social Work and a minor in Criminal Justice. Raised on the south side of Atlanta, her implementation of progressive work in the areas of substance abuse, sexual health and various intersecting topics actively amplifies the current standing curriculum, training and programming. She has led numerous initiatives, campaigns, panels and conferences. Shakera aims to continue expanding her scope of work by creating a space where a plethora of tools and resources can be found for those in need nationwide.


Q: Why do you serve on the SHLI HIV Equity Task Force?

"I do this work because I believe everyone should have the best opportunity to live the life they have envisioned for themselves with the proper tools, resources and affordable access. Each and every one of us deserves a chance to experience all of life and its wonders with the lens of preparedness, awareness and empathy to pay it forward for the next generation."


Q: What is one of your favorite quotes?

"One individual cannot possibly make a difference, alone. It is individual efforts, collectively, that make a noticeable difference—all the difference in the world!" ~Jane Goodall

Introducing the Georgia Thrives 200 Campaign


National Black HIV/AIDS Awareness Day (NBHAAD) is Friday, February 7. NBHAAD recognizes the disproportionate impact of HIV on Black people. On this day, there is a call to increase awareness and access to HIV prevention, testing, treatment and care. 


According to data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the Southern United States, including Georgia, accounts for the highest rates of new HIV diagnoses in the country. Within this region, rural areas often face significant barriers to healthcare access, including limited testing facilities and healthcare providers, which exacerbates the risk for students at Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) in these communities. This lack of access to comprehensive sexual health education and prevention resources contributes to higher vulnerability and the continued spread of the virus among young adults, particularly those attending HBCUs in rural settings.


The Satcher Health Leadership Institute at Morehouse School of Medicine is answering the call by taking it to the streets of rural Georgia with its Georgia Thrives 200 Campaign. The two-day event on February 6 and 7 is focused on providing home-testing kits to 200 students and staff at Fort Valley State University in middle Georgia.
